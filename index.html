Principle 1 of 9
Cut dummy's ruffs
Dummy is short in hearts and has only three small trumps. Declarer's plan is to ruff his heart losers there. Each trump we lead takes away one of those ruffs.
When dummy is short and has few trumps, lead trumps. Lead another every time you get in.

Principle 2 of 9
Don't catch partner in the middle
When you lead hearts, dummy plays last. If partner plays the king, dummy's ace takes it. If partner plays low, dummy wins cheaply.
Don't lead into a tenace that sits over partner. Usually, return partner's suit instead.

Principle 3 of 9
Hold up against dummy's long suit
Dummy has a long, strong suit and no other way in. Declarer has to use his own diamonds to reach it. Take your ace too early and he still has one left to get there.
Count declarer's cards in the suit, then hold up your ace until he has none left.

Principle 4 of 9
Count, then hold up twice
Holding up only works if you hold up long enough. Dummy has five diamonds, you have three, and partner's high-low shows two. So declarer started with three.
Keep ducking until declarer is out of the suit, however many rounds that takes.

Principle 5 of 9
Take the safe exit
Sometimes you just need to get off lead without giving anything away. A suit like ace-king-queen in dummy, with no extra length, is perfectly safe: those tricks are declarer's anyway.
When you need a passive lead, find the suit where you can't lose anything.

Principle 6 of 9
Cash out before the discards
Dummy has a solid long suit. Once trumps are drawn, declarer will throw his losers on it. There's no time to be passive: count the tricks you need and take them now.
When dummy has a source of discards, cash your tricks first.

Principle 7 of 9
No ruff and discard
When declarer and dummy are both out of a suit, leading it is almost always a gift. Declarer ruffs in one hand and throws a loser from the other.
Before leading a suit, check both hands. If both are void, find another card.

Principle 8 of 9
Force declarer with your winners
Declarer is out of hearts and you hold the top ones. Dummy has to follow, so each heart makes declarer ruff in his own hand and shortens his trumps.
When you have length in trumps, make declarer ruff.

Principle 9 of 9
Set up an uppercut
The exception to principle 7. Partner is out of clubs but dummy still has one, so this isn't a ruff and discard. Lead a high club so partner knows to ruff as high as possible.
A high ruff by partner can create trump tricks for you.
